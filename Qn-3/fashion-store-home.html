The Edit

Notes from the studio
"Every seam is a decision. We just try to make fewer of them."

This season began with a single length of raw wool and a question: how little can a garment need before it stops working. What came back was a collection built on structure rather than ornament — coats that hold their own shape, trousers cut wide enough to move in, and a palette lifted straight from wet asphalt and low winter light.

Every piece is made to be worn out of order. The blazer belongs over the dress as much as under the coat. The trouser reads as formal on Monday and off-duty by the weekend. Nothing here was designed to be styled only one way, because nobody actually gets dressed only one way.

We kept the run small on purpose. Once a piece sells through, it does not come back in the same fabric twice. That is not scarcity for its own sake — it is just how the studio has always worked, one bolt of cloth at a time, until there is no more of it left to cut.
